Wrong state means 409 Conflict. For example: approving a DRAFT, editing while SUBMITTED, or doing anything at all to an APPROVED or REJECTED application.

Risk status

An application has two separate status fields:

  • Application status: where it is in the business workflow (the state machine above).
  • Risk status: what the background risk check is doing. When it's COMPLETED, a risk level of LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH is stored too.

Merging them would create states like SUBMITTED_RISK_PENDING and SUBMITTED_RISK_FAILED, and the state machine would get messy.

The risk score never decides on its own. A HIGH result doesn't auto-reject; the reviewer always makes the call. Approving a HIGH-risk application requires a written justification.

Business rules

  1. Editing is only possible in DRAFT and INFO_REQUESTED, so a risk result always matches the data that was checked.
  2. Drafts are always complete. Everything is validated on create and update, so submit only checks state and permissions. The trade-off: a half-filled form can't be saved.
  3. Notes are required for reject, request info, and approving a HIGH-risk application.
  4. The risk score never auto-decides. The reviewer always makes the call.
  5. Assigning: allowed in SUBMITTED, IN_REVIEW and INFO_REQUESTED. The person assigned must be an active REVIEWER. An unassigned application in IN_REVIEW can't be decided until an ADMIN assigns someone.
  6. Submissions are numbered (1, 2, …). Each submit or resubmit runs a fresh risk check, and the number is the idempotency key.
  7. Final means final. Nothing can happen to APPROVED or REJECTED applications. A rejected merchant applies again with a new application; the old one stays in history.
  8. Owners: 1 to 10 per application, total ownership at most 100%, exactly one primary contact. Owners are only changed through their application, while it's editable.
  9. Applications are never deleted. Payment companies must keep history.
  10. Applications an ADMIN creates belong to that ADMIN.
  11. Simultaneous actions: only one wins; the other gets 409 (see Concurrency).
